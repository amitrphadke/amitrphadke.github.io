I sat the Claude Certified Developer – Foundations exam on Tuesday 3 November and passed it. This is the last entry in a journal that was supposed to run every Sunday from September and instead has two posts in it, which tells you most of what follows.

<!--more-->

## The plan did not survive contact

The plan was nine chapters over sixty days at ninety minutes a day: read, build a lab, review on Sunday. By the middle of October I had finished Chapter 0 and one video course. Everything else was untouched. Work, a factory order and a family trip all landed in the same six weeks, and the honest reason is simpler than any of those: a plan that needs ninety uninterrupted minutes every single day has no slack in it, and the first week I missed became the week I avoided opening the tracker at all.

With eighteen days left I had to decide between moving the exam and triaging what was left. I held the date and cut hard.

## What the last three weeks actually were

Three things, in order of how much they mattered.

**Weighting by the blueprint rather than by the syllabus.** Applications & Integration is 33.1% of the paper, Model Selection 16.8%, Agents & Workflows 14.7%. Two thirds of the exam sits in three domains. Claude Code and Eval together are 5.7% and got one shared afternoon. Spending equal time per chapter, which is what my original plan did, is the most expensive mistake available.

**Dropping the labs.** This was the painful one. I had thirty-three hand-written labs with tests that ticked themselves off in the tracker when they passed, and they cost about ten of my remaining twenty-seven hours. The exam is multiple choice. They are still in the repository and I am working through them now, which is the right order for me even though it is the wrong order for learning.

**Daily mock tests with the reasoning attached.** This is the thing I would do from day one if I started again. Twenty questions a day, weighted like the real paper, and the moment you commit to an answer you get the reasoning for all four options — not just why the right one is right, but why each wrong one is tempting and where it breaks. Anything missed comes back the next day, then after two days, then four. Three weeks of that taught me more than the six weeks of reading that preceded it, and it is the only part of the whole setup I would call genuinely well designed.

## What I would tell myself in September

Start with the questions, not the material. Retrieval practice from day one, with the reading used to patch what the questions expose, instead of reading everything first and testing at the end. Build the plan around the exam weights from the start. And leave slack: a plan that only works if you never miss a day is a plan that fails on the first bad week.

## What is still up

The [study tracker](/learning/ccdv-f/tracker/) is unchanged — the day-by-day plan, the practice bank, the daily mock with every option explained, the spaced-repetition trap deck, the cheat sheet and sixty-two digests of the documentation the exam draws on. The [mind maps](/learning/ccdv-f/mindmaps/) are there, as interactive maps and as FreeMind files. The labs are in `labs/` in this site's repository with a setup guide. There is also a [cram sheet](/learning/ccdv-f/cram/) I wrote the morning of the exam, which condenses all eight domains into something you can read in ninety minutes, and which I would not recommend as a study strategy but will admit was useful.
